# Lesson quality rubric (draft for review)

> This is NOT a template. These are **principles** the critic will use to review each lesson and
> request improvements. The idea is to give judgment, not rigid rails. Read it and tell me what to
> remove, change, or add.

## What makes a good lesson (the north star)

A lesson teaches **one competency** in the way that best fits THAT content and THAT person. There
is no single form: content of different natures (a procedure, an abstract concept, a physical
skill, a data analysis) deserves different experiences. The system should not know specific
domains; it reasons about the **nature of the content**, not the topic.

---

## Principles (what the critic checks)

### 1. Interaction has to ADD VALUE, not be a toll
- Interacting should make you think, decide, or attempt **before** seeing the answer.
- ❌ Bad: hiding information the learner should read directly, behind a click (a list of
  informational steps stuffed into a reveal).
- ✅ Good: a worked example you reveal step by step **in order to reason** through the next one.
- Rule: if hiding something doesn't force thinking, **don't hide it**.

### 2. The right component for the content
- Simple sequential info → show it in full (visible list of steps), not a reveal.
- Example to reason through → progressive worked example.
- Compare two states → before/after. Order things → drag. Data → chart/table.
- Concept with a key term → glossary/flashcard.
- Rule: the component is chosen for **what the learner has to do**, not to fill space.

### 3. Several screens when the content calls for it (no fixed number)
- A simple concept can be 1 screen; one with practice, 2-3; a dense one, more.
- Each screen is understood on its own, centered, **with no scroll**.
- The content decides, never "always N screens".

### 4. Always interactive, but not everything is a test
- Every lesson has at least one real interaction.
- Interacting ≠ evaluating. Most of it can be exploration/play; evaluate only when there is
  something that can genuinely be checked.
- The last screen **does not block** progress (it is noted/logged, but you can continue).

### 5. Faithful to the source, honest about mastery
- Everything comes from the document/pack. No inventing facts, interfaces, or scenarios the
  source does not have.
- If it cannot really be evaluated (there is no oracle), the lesson is still interactive but
  **does not certify mastery** (it is support). It never fakes a test.

### 6. Adapted to the person
- Adjusts support/pace based on the learner's level and errors.
- Designed to grow: tomorrow, adaptation for accessibility/capabilities without rebuilding
  anything.

### 7. No noise
- No filler text, no content from another domain, no artifacts ("[Source: …]").
- Regular text reads as regular text (not everything in giant "lead" style).

---

## What the critic does with this

After generating a lesson, the critic (a different perspective) checks it against these principles
and leaves **concrete**, actionable notes ("this is info, don't hide it behind a reveal"; "drag fits
here, not a quiz"; "this is 2 screens, not 1"). The generator adjusts **once**. It doesn't impose
numbers or templates: judgment nudges.
